The Office of the Director-General began transmitting and handling correspondence electronically as a deliberate move to make the Agency’s own operations smart and efficient — in line with the mandate it holds for everyone else. This is how far that has come.

The climb

Five levels of automation, reached one at a time

Each level was earned before the next was attempted. Nothing was skipped, and every level still runs — the earlier ones did not stop working, they stopped being the only option.

The dashed line is the point of the whole exercise: as each level was reached, the human effort spent on each document fell — while the checks did not. Level five is not automation replacing judgement; it is automation carrying the routine so that judgement is applied where it matters, with human-in-the-loop checks and controls at every decision.

The process

Four ways in. One governed path through.

Whatever channel a document arrives by, it becomes the same record and travels the same six stages — watched from above, and visible below to the person who sent it.

Operations

How work is actually controlled

Automation without control is just faster disorder. Every document passes five gates, and at each one something is enforced, something is written to the record, and there is a named path for when it does not go to plan.

Gate 1

Registration

Enforced

A reference is minted once. Category and subcategory are set on arrival.

Recorded

Responsible unit, supporting unit, priority and both SLA clocks — derived, not typed.

Exception

Unrecognised category lands on a named default, never on whatever sorts first.

Gate 2

Acknowledgement

Enforced

Receipt must be confirmed before work starts, within the acknowledgement window.

Recorded

A receipt in the ledger: who, when, from where, and the idempotency key that stops a double entry.

Exception

Not acknowledged in time → it ages into the SLA watch list.

Gate 3

Action

Enforced

Work happens in one queue, against the record, with the completion clock in view.

Recorded

Comments, updates and evidence attach to the record as the officer works.

Exception

Waiting on something outside the unit → blocked, and visibly so.

Gate 4

Decision

Enforced

Approve, return, reject or escalate — a decision is one of four things, not an opinion in an email.

Recorded

The decision, the decider and the trail behind it, available to audit.

Exception

Beyond the unit’s authority → escalated to the executive decision hub.

Gate 5

Dispatch & closure

Enforced

Outward correspondence goes on a branded template; closure requires a dispatch outcome.

Recorded

Dispatch receipt, closure, then archive under a retention class with a retention date.

Exception

No-dispatch is an outcome too — recorded with its reason, not left open.

How it was built, and what comes next

A frontend-for-backend strategy — and the phase after it

The approach was right for the phase it served. It is also honest to say what that phase deliberately deferred, and what the next one must fund.

What it bought

Capability without waiting, and without the bill

Rather than wait for backend infrastructure that had not been procured, the platform was built front-first — the interface carrying the logic, the governance and the integration, calling automation flows directly.

  • ✓No servers, no framework, no middleware to license or maintain.
  • ✓Deployed as static files — nothing to keep alive between the interface and the flows.
  • ✓The gap in backend infrastructure was closed rather than waited on.
  • ✓Five levels of automation reached on operating budget, not a capital programme.
What the next phase needs

From a correspondence system to enterprise content management

The ERP–ECM Charter already sets the boundary and names the destination: a governed content estate alongside the transaction systems, not inside them. That destination needs backend investment the current phase deliberately deferred.

  • →A governed repository with versioning, publication and disposition — not only correspondence, but contracts, policies, project records and evidence.
  • →Records controls and legal hold administered enterprise-wide, with retention classes enforced rather than declared.
  • →Linkage to ERP on shared identifiers — purchase order, invoice, contract, project — so a transaction and its evidence find each other.
  • →Storage, search and integration capacity sized for the whole Agency, not one office.
The next rung

Opening the channel to the public

Every level so far has improved how the Agency handles correspondence once it arrives. The sixth extends the same governed record outward — to the person who sent it.

Built and answering

A citizen submits, verifies, tracks and responds — without a visit or a phone call

The outward-facing channel is built and its endpoints answer. Connecting it to the automation layer is a configuration step, not a build. What follows is the first submission that travels the full path on the record.

  1. Submit from anywhere, with attachments Identity confirmed by a one-time code to the sender’s own address before anything enters the registry.
  2. Receive a reference, and watch it move Received, in review, action required, closed — the same states the officers work in, shown to the person waiting.
  3. Respond, add a note, or withdraw The correspondence stays a conversation rather than a one-way deposit.
  4. Close the loop on the record Dispatch receipt, closure and retention — the citizen sees the outcome, and the Agency keeps the evidence.
